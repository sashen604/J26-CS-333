## Explanation stability under small valid perturbations

50 benign + 50 Mirai test rows (seed 0), 10 perturbations per row, top-k with k = 5. Each perturbation multiplies every continuous feature by an independent factor in [0.95, 1.05]. Protocol codes and 0–1 shares are not perturbed, known upper bounds are clipped, and quantised features are re-rounded to 0.1. LIME uses a fixed seed and 5,000 samples per explanation, with sampling statistics from 5,000 train rows. Definitions are in `xai/metrics.py`.

**Citation:** perturbation stability per Gan et al., "Is your explanation stable?": A Robustness Evaluation Framework for Feature Attribution (ACM CCS 2022). The Intersection score uses E-XAI's formula (Arreche et al., IEEE Access 2024, Sec. C.4), adapted to perturbed inputs. Jaccard and Spearman are our additions. (The CCS 2022 paper's own stability metric, mstd, is not computed here.)

- **Jaccard:** mean overlap of the top-5 set with the unperturbed row's top-5.
- **Spearman:** mean rank correlation of all attributions with the unperturbed row's attributions.
- **Intersection:** the share of the unperturbed top-5 that stays in the top-5 under every perturbation.

Values are mean ± std over rows (sample std). All metrics are 1.0 for a perfectly stable explanation.

## E-XAI repeatability (Arreche et al.)

E-XAI's original stability (IEEE Access 2024, Sec. C.4): explain the same 100 UNCHANGED rows N = 3 times each and report |X_1 ∩ X_2 ∩ X_3| / k for the top-5. LIME runs with its normal random sampling: it is not reseeded between runs, and the explainer is seeded once at construction so the script is reproducible. SHAP TreeExplainer is deterministic, so its score is 1.0 by construction.

| Method | Class | Repeatability |
|---|---|---|
| SHAP | benign | 1.000 ± 0.000 |
| SHAP | Mirai | 1.000 ± 0.000 |
| LIME | benign | 0.648 ± 0.103 |
| LIME | Mirai | 0.564 ± 0.132 |

## Findings

- **`Header_Length` leads SHAP at 27.6% vs 88.7% of XGBoost gain.** Gain adds up the loss reduction from every split on a feature, so the feature chosen for the large early splits collects most of it. SHAP divides each individual prediction among all the features that moved it, so features used in later, smaller splits (`Max`, `Std`) still receive substantial credit.
- **Flip asymmetry:** under ±5% valid perturbations, 28/500 benign vs 0/500 Mirai perturbations changed the predicted class. Some benign predictions sit close to the decision boundary, while Mirai predictions do not move.
- **`Std` is the #3 most influential feature (17.3% of mean |SHAP|)** and keeps its sample-size dependence (spread over 10 vs 100 packets). This is a documented limitation in `data/README.md`, pending the PCAP feature recompute.
